Assuming the Best in People Leads to Win-Win Situations

Claudia Beck

October 19

Assuming the best in people, also known as a positive or optimistic outlook in human interactions, has been the subject of various studies and research in the fields of psychology, sociology, and management. When we look for positive intent, we give people the benefit of the doubt and we give ourselves the chance to learn the details of the situation.

We all make mistakes. However, we hold a double standard — we judge other people's mistakes differently than we judge our own. When we make mistakes, we often blame the circumstances of the situation rather than take responsibility. When other people make mistakes, we tend to over-emphasize the other person's role — we very quickly blame them. This double standard is well documented in scientific research and is called the Fundamental Attribution Error.

Imagine this scenario: An employee named Charly didn't deliver his sales target for the past three months, while he had a stable and consistent positive track record over the past few years. In Scenario 1, the manager focuses on performance metrics and outcomes. In Scenario 2, the manager takes a more empathetic approach, focusing on understanding Charly's personal challenges and assumes positive intent.

Scenario 1: Manager Issues a Warning

The manager tells Charly that if his performance doesn't improve, he will be let go. Potential outcomes:

  • —

    Immediate Impact on Charly: Charly may feel stressed, pressured, or demotivated by the threat of job loss, further affecting his performance negatively.

  • —

    Lack of Understanding: The manager may miss the opportunity to address potential issues that could be resolved.

  • —

    Employee Morale: This approach could negatively impact the morale of other employees who witness Charly's situation, creating a culture of fear and insecurity.

  • —

    Legal and Ethical Concerns: This approach could raise concerns, especially if Charly is facing personal challenges outside his control.

Scenario 2: Manager Shows Empathy

The manager takes a more empathetic approach, focusing on understanding Charly's personal challenges. Potential outcomes:

  • —

    Open Communication: Charly may feel valued and understood, which can lead to more open communication about his personal challenges.

  • —

    Resolution of Issues: By discussing his personal challenges, Charly and his manager may identify potential solutions or accommodations that could help him overcome the difficulties he's facing.

  • —

    Positive Work Environment: This approach contributes to a positive work environment where employees feel supported and that their well-being is a priority.

  • —

    Long-Term Performance Improvement: By addressing the root causes of Charly's performance decline, there is a better chance for sustained, long-term improvement.

  • —

    Legal and Ethical Compliance: This approach aligns with ethical and legal obligations, ensuring that employees are not unfairly treated when facing personal challenges.

The second scenario is more likely to lead to a holistic understanding of the situation, potentially resulting in a more effective and compassionate resolution. The first scenario, while focusing on performance metrics, may miss the opportunity to address underlying problems and could create a negative work environment.

How Assuming the Best Influences Performance

Assuming the best in people, or having a positive and optimistic approach to interactions in the workplace, can have a profound impact on employee performance:

  • 01

    Trust and Rapport Building: When leaders assume the best in their interactions, it fosters trust and builds rapport — the foundation of effective working relationships.

  • 02

    Positive Work Culture: A workplace culture that assumes the best is generally more positive, inclusive, and supportive, leading to higher performance.

  • 03

    Motivation: When employees sense that their ideas and contributions are valued and their intentions trusted, they are more motivated to excel in their roles.

  • 04

    Open Communication: An assumption of the best encourages open and constructive communication, and employees are more likely to voice their concerns and offer suggestions.

  • 05

    Reduced Conflicts: When individuals assume positive intentions, they are less likely to jump to negative conclusions, reducing workplace tensions.

  • 06

    Team Collaboration: Assuming the best promotes collaboration within teams. When team members trust and respect each other, they are more likely to work cohesively and effectively.

  • 07

    Innovation and Risk-Taking: A culture of trust encourages employees to take calculated risks and explore innovative ideas, leading to creative problem-solving.

  • 08

    Job Satisfaction: Employees who feel valued and respected in the workplace are generally more satisfied with their jobs, leading to increased motivation and better performance.

  • 09

    Lower Turnover: A positive, trusting workplace culture reduces turnover rates. Employees are more likely to stay with an organization where they feel valued.

  • 10

    Customer and Client Relations: Employees who assume the best are more likely to deliver better customer service, positively impacting the organization's reputation.

In Summary

Assuming the best in people creates a more positive and trusting work environment. It fosters a more supportive and collaborative environment that encourages personal growth and improved performance. Employees are more likely to excel in their roles when they feel respected and trusted, as it leads to higher morale, productivity, and job satisfaction.

By embracing this approach, organizations can promote the well-being of their employees while achieving their performance goals.
