## SECTION 2 — WHAT IT IS

*No section label. Prose. Sets up the framework before introducing the dimensions.*

> Most leadership frameworks describe what good leaders do.
>
> ATTUNE describes what gets in the way.
>
> The unexamined reaction before a high-stakes conversation. The tone that closes a room before a word is spoken. The trust that was never built because the hard conversation was never had. The silence that was mistaken for agreement. The dynamic that everyone in the room can feel but nobody will name. The leader who is performing under pressure instead of leading through it.
>
> These are not character flaws. They are patterns — visible, learnable, and changeable.
>
> Six dimensions. Each one measurable. Each one with a direct line to how decisions get made, how execution moves, and whether the people in the room trust each other enough to do their best work.

## SECTION 3 — THE SIX DIMENSIONS

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> THE SIX DIMENSIONS

**Intro line:**
> Each dimension operates on a spectrum: Strength (consistent and reliable under pressure), Developing (present but not yet reliable), or Growth Edge (the place where pressure creates the most cost).

---

### A — AWARENESS
**Subtitle:** Self-regulation before response

> **Leaders who react from unexamined triggers lose authority faster than those who pause.**
>
> Every high-stakes conversation is shaped by what the leader carries into it — unresolved pressure, competing priorities, fear that has not been named. Awareness is the discipline of noticing that before it shapes what happens in the room.
>
> Strong reactions are rarely about the moment. They almost always signal something beneath it.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *What am I carrying into this conversation that does not belong here?*

---

### T — TONE
**Subtitle:** How you communicate shapes every relationship

> **The emotional tone you carry enters the room before you speak a word.**
>
> Regulated leaders build relationship capital. Reactive leaders spend it faster than they earn it. Tone is not about warmth or formality — it is about whether the climate you create makes people more or less able to think clearly, speak honestly, and act with confidence.
>
> Communication that is calm and direct builds the relational foundation that execution depends on.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *What is my tone communicating right now — and is it creating clarity or noise?*

---

### T — TRUST
**Subtitle:** The architecture of honest conversation

> **Speed of execution is directly proportional to the level of trust in the room.**
>
> Leaders who avoid difficult conversations believe they are protecting the relationship. They are not. They are deferring its erosion. The absence of honest conversation does not preserve trust — it quietly drains it, until the moment when the drain becomes visible and the cost is already high.
>
> Trust is not built through agreement. It is built through the willingness to say the hard thing and remain in the relationship afterward.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *Are people able to tell me the hard thing? If not — why not?*

---

### U — UNDERSTANDING
**Subtitle:** The quality of listening shapes every relationship

> **The presenting issue is rarely the real issue.**
>
> What goes unread beneath behavior — the fear, the misalignment, the pressure that has no outlet — becomes political drag. It surfaces in side conversations, passive resistance, decisions that seem inexplicable until you understand what the person making them was actually responding to.
>
> The most expensive leadership blind spot is mistaking silence for agreement. Relationships erode in that gap.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *What is the real issue underneath what I am being told?*

---

### N — NAMING
**Subtitle:** Clarity as a leadership act

> **Unspoken dynamics do not disappear. They surface as disengagement, politics, or poor decisions.**
>
> Naming what others avoid is not a confrontation — it is the communication act that builds the deepest professional trust. The leaders who can say the hard thing clearly, without blame, and without withdrawing from the relationship afterward accelerate every process around them.
>
> What the room cannot name, the room cannot resolve.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *What truth in this room am I avoiding naming — and what is that costing?*

---

### E — EQUILIBRIUM
**Subtitle:** Stability under pressure

> **Equilibrium is not the absence of pressure. It is the capacity to lead clearly inside it.**
>
> Under sustained pressure, even skilled leaders begin to lead from the pressure rather than through it. Decisions become defensive. Preparation becomes excessive. The instinct that was once an asset begins to override judgment.
>
> Relationships survive difficulty not because conflict is avoided, but because repair is done well. The response to a difficult moment is always more visible — and more consequential — than the moment itself.

*Diagnostic question:*
> *Am I leading from equilibrium right now — or from the pressure itself?*

## SECTION 4 — TWO LEVELS OF APPLICATION

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> WHERE THE FRAMEWORK APPLIES

> ATTUNE was developed through individual executive work. But every dimension describes something that happens between people, not just within them.
>
> The same framework that surfaces an individual leader's blind spots surfaces a leadership team's collective ones.

**Two columns:**

### The Individual Leader

> Each dimension is a lens on your own leadership behavior under pressure. The diagnostic reveals where you operate from strength and where pressure creates patterns that cost you — in authority, in trust, in execution quality.
>
> The work is interior: notice what is happening, regulate the response, and choose how to act from a clearer place.

### The Leadership Team

> The same dimensions describe how a team functions as a collective. A team's Awareness: does this team see its own dynamics clearly, or does it keep arriving at the same friction points? A team's Trust: is there enough safety to say the thing everyone is thinking? A team's Equilibrium: is this team making decisions from strategic clarity — or from organizational pressure?
>
> When the ATTUNE framework is applied to a leadership team, the diagnostic becomes a map of where collective human dynamics are creating the most execution drag.
