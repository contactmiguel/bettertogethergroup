## SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM THIS SOLVES

*Design: Light background. Short, precise. Names the specific isolation that senior leaders feel — the thing they rarely admit.*

> At a certain level of seniority, the weight of consequential decisions becomes quietly isolating.
>
> The decisions with the highest stakes — the ones that carry financial, reputational, or organizational consequence — are often the ones you cannot fully think through with your team, your peers inside the organization, or even your board. The political dynamics are too present. The vulnerabilities too exposed. The need to appear decided too strong.
>
> Most leaders navigate this alone. The Strategic Leadership Council is built for those who would rather not.

---

## SECTION 3 — WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT

*Design: Navy background. Three differentiation points — each a short, direct statement followed by one sentence of context. Not a feature list. A set of clear distinctions.*

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> NOT A MASTERMIND. NOT A PEER GROUP. NOT GROUP COACHING.

**Point 1:**
> **Structured, not open.**
> Every session is facilitated through the ATTUNE framework. Discussions move from surface tension into the dynamics beneath — and end in clarity and specific commitments, not in shared perspective.

**Point 2:**
> **Diagnostic, not advisory.**
> Members do not come to give each other advice. They come to examine their own thinking under pressure, surface what they have been avoiding, and make better decisions because of what the room surfaced — not because of what the room told them.

**Point 3:**
> **Confidential by design.**
> What is brought into the room stays in the room. The Council functions because every member knows that what they say here will not appear in a conversation elsewhere — including with Claudia.

## SECTION 4 — HOW IT WORKS

*Design: Light background. Clean numbered sequence — four elements. Shorter than the Retreat arc, more intimate in tone.*

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> THE STRUCTURE

**⚑ Note: Details below are a strong working draft. Claudia to confirm: session length, cadence, group size, in-person vs. virtual format.**

**01 — A small cohort**
> ⚑ [6–8 members] per cohort. Senior leaders only — C-suite, SVP, and equivalent. Deliberately small so every member has genuine airtime and genuine accountability to the group.

**02 — Quarterly convenings**
> ⚑ [Four sessions per year, each a full day / half-day]. Members bring real business tensions as the working agenda — not prepared presentations, not case studies. The situation they are actually navigating right now.

**03 — ATTUNE-facilitated sessions**
> Each session is structured around the ATTUNE framework. Claudia facilitates — not as a moderator, but as a diagnostic lens. The conversation moves from what is presented to what is actually at stake, to what has been going unsaid, to what the member most needs to decide before they leave the room.

**04 — Commitments, not insights**
> Sessions end with specific commitments — what each member will do differently, what decision they will make, what conversation they will have. The Council is not a thinking space. It is a deciding space.

## SECTION 5 — WHAT MEMBERS BRING

*Design: White background. Three examples of the kinds of tensions members work through — specific enough to be recognizable, anonymized enough to feel safe. These replace a generic "who it's for" list.*

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> WHAT GETS WORKED ON

> The tensions Council members bring are not hypothetical. They are the ones most leaders carry quietly for weeks before acting — or before failing to act.

**Example 1:**
> A CEO considering a significant restructuring who needs to think through the decision before announcing it — including what she has been telling herself about the reasons that may not be the full picture.

**Example 2:**
> A senior executive whose relationship with a key board member has become quietly adversarial, and who has not yet found a way to name it — or decide what to do about it.

**Example 3:**
> A leader whose team is performing but whose own engagement has been declining for months, and who needs a room that is safe enough to say that out loud and think through what it means.

> These are not problems to be solved by advice. They are decisions to be made by the leader who carries them — with the benefit of structure, rigour, and people who are operating at the same level and have no stake in the outcome.

## SECTION 6 — WHO THIS IS FOR

*Design: Light grey background. Brief, precise. The self-selection criteria are narrower here than on any other page — that is intentional and should feel intentional.*

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> MEMBERSHIP

> The Strategic Leadership Council is for a specific kind of leader — not the most senior by title, but the most willing to examine their own thinking honestly.

> Members tend to share a few things in common:

- They operate with real consequence — financial, organizational, or reputational risk is attached to their decisions
- They have reached a level where the honest conversations are harder to find inside the organization
- They are willing to bring what is actually difficult, not what is presentable
- They understand that the most valuable thing in the room is not the advice of peers, but the clarity that comes from examining their own thinking rigorously

> **⚑ [Application-based entry.] Membership is by invitation or application.** The selection process is designed to ensure the group has the coherence and trust necessary for the work to be real.

## SECTION 7 — A NOTE ON CONFIDENTIALITY

*Design: White background. Short, standalone section. This is the unspoken objection for every buyer at this level — address it directly.*

**Section label (small caps, gold):**
> ON CONFIDENTIALITY

> Senior leaders considering the Council have one question they often do not ask directly: *will what I say here stay here?*
>
> The answer is yes — fully and without exception. What members bring to the Council remains in the Council. This includes Claudia. The session is not a coaching engagement. There are no notes shared, no summaries sent, no debrief with a sponsor or HR.
>
> The Council functions because members trust the room completely. That trust is protected by design, not by policy.

## DESIGN NOTES

- This is the most text-forward page on the site by intent — the offer requires trust, and trust requires depth. Visual restraint is appropriate here.
- Section 3 (What Makes It Different) should use the navy background with three distinct visual blocks — each differentiation point separated clearly. Not a list. Three statements.
- Section 5 (What Gets Worked On) is the most human section on the page — the three examples should feel like they were written by someone who has sat in those conversations. No icons, no cards, just clean prose.
- Section 7 (Confidentiality) should feel like a deliberate pause — different visual weight from the surrounding sections. Consider a subtle gold left border treatment to signal that this is a direct address to the reader.
- The closing CTA copy "Membership is limited. Cohorts form when the right group is ready." is doing important work — it signals exclusivity and removes the pressure of immediate commitment simultaneously.
